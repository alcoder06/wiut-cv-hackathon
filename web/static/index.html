Events as segments

Stopped vehicles, congestion, jaywalking, failure to yield, red light, stop line, near misses and more: each with a start and end that follow the annotators' rules.

Warning before a crash

Frame by frame, from past frames only: time-to-collision between every pair of road users plus sudden braking, as a risk score from 0 to 1.

Learns the road

Where the carriageway is and which way each lane flows are learned from the traffic itself, so parked cars and people on the pavement never count as traffic.

Architecture

Problem & approach

Part A runs offline, so it may use the whole video: it smooths tracks in both directions and backdates event starts. Part B must use past frames only: it runs its own detector and tracker and never reads Part A's output.

01

Detect

YOLO11s, COCO-pretrained, not fine-tuned. 8 frames per second, 960 px input, FP16 on GPU, batches of 16.

02

ByteTrack links boxes into tracks. Speed is measured in box sizes per second at the ground point, so one threshold works near and far.

No external training data. We had no labels for this camera, four days, and a hidden test set with events that are not in the samples. Rules on trajectories carry over to unseen events of a known type; a model trained on other cameras' crashes would not have matched this view in that time.

Why these choices

  • YOLO11s at 960 px: small, distant pedestrians need the resolution; it is the largest model that keeps 4K inside the time budget with margin.
  • Speed in box sizes per second: no camera calibration, and one threshold works across the frame.
  • Precision over recall: the score averages F1 over event types, and a type we predict that is not in the test set adds a zero. road_obstacle, fire_smoke and wrong_way stay off.
  • Boundaries follow the annotation rules: e.g. stopped_vehicle starts when the car stops, not 10 s later when the rule is confirmed. Events are matched by temporal IoU up to 0.7.
  • Time safety valves: each part caps its own time, so a slow machine loses a few events instead of scoring zero for the video.

Part B: warning before a crash

Every 0.2 s: detect and track (own instance), then for every pair of road users solve when two discs at their ground points would touch. Pairs are dropped if they are not closing fast (under 1 size/s), are parallel in neighbouring lanes, or one is a stopped car being brushed past. A pair must stay threatening for 3 samples. The earliest time-to-collision maps to risk with a logistic (0.5 at 1.5 s), sharp braking raises it, and a moving average smooths it. Detection runs in a worker thread with a fixed one-sample lag, so the curve is identical run to run.

Engineering

  • The model loads when solution.py is imported, before the harness starts its per-video clock.
  • Measured with the official harness on a laptop RTX 3050: 0.34× (1080p) to 1.32× (4K) of video length across the 4 samples, against a 3× limit. Without a GPU, on 8 CPU cores: 1.45×.
  • Decoding in a background thread, skipped frames only grabbed, exact halvings to 960 px: 787 s → 460 s.
  • Seeds fixed, cuDNN deterministic, fixed frame stride: two runs give the same file.
  • Detections cached during development, so a rule change is re-scored in seconds.
  • 12 unit tests on synthetic trajectories cover each rule and the risk filters.

Findings that shaped the solution

  • 4K at 29.97 fps, 5.8 GB for 5 minutes. Decoding alone ate most of the time budget: decoding moved to a background thread, skipped frames are only grabbed, and the detector sees 960 px made by exact halvings (787 s → 460 s on 8 cores).
  • Cars at the bottom of the frame are 4× the size of cars at the top (median box 424 px vs 107 px on C3897). Speeds in pixels meant nothing across the frame, so every speed is in box sizes per second.
  • The signal cycle dominates the scene (the saw-tooth above). A stopped car only counts as stopped_vehicle if traffic around it keeps moving; otherwise it is a queue.
  • Cars park along the left kerb and never move. The carriageway is learned only from moving vehicles, so parked cars fall outside it.
  • Two carriageways, opposite directions. A grid of dominant directions learned from trajectories splits congestion by direction. We also built wrong_way on it, but on our labels it found 0 real cases and 1 false one, so it is switched off.
  • Pedestrians cross wide crossings in groups, often diagonally. "Anyone on the crossing" fired on almost every car; failure_to_yield now needs a pedestrian near the car's path.
  • The gantry lights face sideways and are unreadable; the left-pole lamp is readable at 4K. Red-light detection reads that lamp and corrects for its measured offset from the boulevard's own phase.

Honest failure cases

Small, reused labels
The team judged 93 detections on the samples (37 real). Tuning on them raised Score A on those samples from 0.53 to 0.74, but it was tuned and scored on the same labels: expect less on the hidden set.
failure_to_yield is the loudest class
Pedestrians waiting at the kerb of a 7-lane crossing were its main false alarm; the rule now needs a walking pedestrian near the car's path. F1 on our labels went from 0.56 to 0.67.
Near misses in dense turning traffic
9 of 13 rejected near misses were cars turning past each other. The rule now ignores vehicles turning more than 30°: F1 0.31 → 0.57 on our labels, still the weakest class.
Missed a real crash
No sample video contains a crash. On a public roadside crash clip (TU-DAT), both the accident rule and Part B missed a side-swipe: the tracker lost the car that spun and gave it a new id, and Part B treats side-by-side cars as normal lane-keeping, the filter that removed most false alarms on our camera.
Long merged segments
On C3896 one stopped_vehicle runs 153 s and one failure_to_yield 62 s, because overlapping events of one type merge into one segment. If the annotators split them, these will not match.
Tracker id switches
In dense queues a stationary car can split into two tracks. stopped_vehicle re-joins gaps under 3 s, but a long occlusion by a bus still splits the event.

Technical report

What we built, what worked, what did not, and what we would do next.

1. What we built

A fixed-camera traffic analyser that reports 9 of the 14 official event types as time segments (Part A) and a per-frame accident risk from past frames only (Part B). It is a pretrained YOLO11s detector with ByteTrack, a scene model learned from the traffic itself, and one rule per event type. No model was trained. It runs inside the organizers' unchanged harness at up to 1.32× video length on a laptop GPU (1.45× on 8 CPU cores alone), against a 3× limit.

2. What worked

  • Trajectory rules cover 9 of 14 classes with no training data, and every event can be traced to a track and a rule.
  • Tuning on 93 detections the team judged raised Score A on the labelled samples from 0.53 to 0.74 (same labels, so optimistic).
  • Learning the road and lane directions from traffic removed most hand drawing and kept parked cars out.
  • Speed in box sizes per second made one set of thresholds work across the whole frame.
  • Filtering Part B by closing speed and lane offset cut false alarms from 35 to 7 in 5 minutes.
  • Threaded decoding and 960 px input cut runtime by 42%.

3. What did not

  • Box overlap as a crash signal: crossing traffic overlaps in the image constantly (22 to 26% of the time in alarm). Replaced by discs at the ground points.
  • "Anyone on the crossing" for failure_to_yield fired on nearly every car.
  • Counting every red-light queue as a stopped vehicle.
  • Reading the gantry signal heads: they face sideways.
  • wrong_way: 0 real and 1 false detection on our labels, so it is off.
  • A real side-swipe on a public crash clip: the tracker lost the car that spun, so the accident rule and Part B both missed it.

4. What we would do next

  • Re-link a track lost near a contact point, so a spinning car keeps its identity through a crash.
  • More labels, so thresholds are tuned on events they are not scored on.
  • Test Part B on public crash footage (CCD, DoTA) and calibrate it so 0.5 means "probably within 5 s".
  • A small clip classifier to confirm accident and near-miss candidates.
  • Fine-tune the detector on frames from this camera for small, distant pedestrians.
  • Run Part B on a live stream, with alerts to an operator.
